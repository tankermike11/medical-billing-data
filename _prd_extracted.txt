Public Data Ingestion Layer
for AI Medical Billing, Claims Advocacy & EOB Explanation
Technical PRD v1.1
Prepared for: Claude Code build
Status: Build-ready  |  Supersedes v1.0
1. Executive summary
This PRD specifies a non-PHI public-data ingestion layer that supplies the reference data required by MASA's AI-assisted medical billing and claims advocacy product. The system normalizes, versions, indexes, and exposes free and publicly accessible healthcare reference data so that downstream AI services can explain EOBs and bills, interpret denial codes, identify a member's plan, look up benefits, surface coverage rules, generate appeal pathways, and ground every user-facing claim in a citable source.
Changes from v1.0: Adds Good Faith Estimate (GFE) provisions of the No Surprises Act (45 CFR §149.610, §149.620) as a new structured-rule source (E.6), in response to use-case analysis showing ~10% of real-world pain centers on quoted-vs-billed price disputes. Names the specific top-10 states for state-scoped families (Medicaid, state appeals, state surprise billing) based on geographic distribution of the use-case dataset: 
TX, FL, CA, GA, MI, NY, NC, PA, AZ, IL. Explicit deferral notes added for full HPT/TiC expansion (non-ambulance), detailed commercial coordination-of-benefits rules, and state Medicaid expansion beyond the top 10. Two adjacent product surfaces are flagged as future PRDs and not built here: an itemized-bill-request workflow (follow-on MVP PRD) and a debt-collection-defense product line (separate strategic PRD).
Scope inherited from v1.0: Plan-side coverage spans ACA Marketplace QHPs, Medicare (Original + Medicare Advantage), Medicaid (top-state subset), and a framework for handling user-uploaded employer-sponsored (ERISA) plan documents. The No Surprises Act and ambulance-specific Medicare sources are P0 because they are directly load-bearing for MASA's ambulance/surprise-bill wedge. A narrow slice of Hospital Price Transparency and payer Transparency in Coverage MRFs—scoped to ambulance HCPCS codes only—is included for negotiated-rate advocacy.
Architectural posture: This system is the public-data layer only. It does not ingest, store, or process PHI. User-uploaded EOBs, bills, and denial letters are handled in a separate PHI-eligible service that consumes this layer's APIs. This separation is load-bearing for HIPAA posture, the deployment-tier model, and the build plan.
Build organization: The data sources are grouped into eight extractor families (A–H), each implementable as a self-contained module with a shared source-registry, normalization, and provenance contract. This structure lets Claude Code build, test, and ship each scraper family independently.
2. Product objective
Build a programmatic data pipeline that ingests, normalizes, indexes, versions, and exposes free and publicly accessible healthcare reference data to support:
Plain-English EOB and bill explanation.
Claim-line interpretation using billing codes (ICD-10-CM, ICD-10-PCS, HCPCS Level II, revenue codes, NDC, place-of-service, modifiers, CARC/RARC denial codes, MS-DRG).
Plan benefit lookup across Marketplace QHPs, Medicare Advantage, Original Medicare, Medicaid, and user-uploaded ESI documents.
Deductible, copay, coinsurance, and out-of-pocket maximum explanation grounded in plan documents.
SBC retrieval, parsing, and structured field extraction.
Plan-material URL discovery (SBC, network, formulary, brochure).
Denial triage and appeal-pathway identification, including federal ACA appeals, state appeal processes, Medicare appeals, Medicaid appeals, and No Surprises Act IDR.
Medical-necessity reasoning grounded in Medicare NCDs/LCDs and top-payer medical policies.
Network/provider verification using NPPES and machine-readable provider directories.
Cost-estimate vs. final-bill disputes for uninsured/self-pay patients (Good Faith Estimate dispute resolution under NSA §300gg-136 / 45 CFR §149.620).
Ambulance-specific advocacy: ambulance HCPCS interpretation, ambulance fee schedules, NCD 10.1 coverage criteria, ambulance modifier interpretation, NSA balance-billing protections, and negotiated-rate context.
CPT carve-out: Full CPT descriptor ingestion is out of scope because CPT is licensed by the AMA. The system supports CPT-like code detection in user documents and maps to broad service categories using free metadata. If a CPT license is later acquired, ingestion can be added without architectural changes.

Debt-collection-defense workflows (FDCPA validation letters, FCRA credit-bureau disputes, CFPB complaint routing) — covered in a separate strategic product PRD currently in scoping. This system is not the right surface for FDCPA/FCRA workflows.
3.5 Top-10 state methodology
The list: TX, FL, CA, GA, MI, NY, NC, PA, AZ, IL.
How it was selected: The product team analyzed 2,848 real-world use cases captured from Reddit billing/finance subreddits and CFPB medical-debt complaints. State extraction was reliable on CFPB data (99.6% coverage) and limited on Reddit data (~3.5% explicit state mention). Composite ranking weighted surprise-billing-themed cases at 2x. The top 10 by weighted volume is the list above.
Caveat for Family E.4 (state surprise billing laws): Of these 10 states, six (CA, NY, TX, FL, IL, PA) have meaningful state-level surprise billing protections that layer on top of or extend NSA. The other four (GA, MI, NC, AZ) are effectively NSA-only jurisdictions for surprise billing. For NSA-only states, Family E.4 ingestion is minimal: record that NSA is the operative framework, mark stronger_than_nsa=false, store a citation to the relevant state DOI summary, and move on. The full per-state-statute work concentrates on the six layered-protection states.
Caveat for Family D (Medicaid) and E.2 (state appeals): All 10 states get full ingestion. Medicaid managed care complexity and state appeal/fair-hearing rule variability are meaningful across all 10.
Validation gate before kickoff: Before Family D, E.2, and E.4 build, the product team should sanity-check this list against actual or anticipated MASA member geographic distribution. If the member-density distribution diverges materially from the use-case-volume distribution, adjust the list. Reddit's poor state-detection coverage means this list is more confident on the 'where complaints come from' axis than on the 'where MASA members are' axis.
Note on follow-on product PRDs: Two adjacent product surfaces were identified during use-case analysis and are NOT built in this PRD. (1) Itemized-bill-request workflow — addresses the largest single user need (~18% of analyzed cases) but is procedural/application-layer, not data ingestion. Covered in a follow-on MVP PRD. (2) Debt-collection-defense product line — addresses ~15% of analyzed cases (the FDCPA/FCRA-flavored CFPB complaint pattern). Covered in a separate strategic PRD currently in scoping. Both ride on this data-ingestion layer for code-decode and rule lookups.
4. Architectural principles
These principles are load-bearing. Deviation requires explicit review.
4.1 PHI separation
This system stores only non-PHI public reference data. User EOBs, bills, denial letters, plan documents uploaded by members, and any data joined to a specific person are processed in a separate PHI-eligible service that consumes this layer's APIs. The two systems run in distinct deployment tiers.
4.2 Provenance is mandatory
Every record exposed by the API must trace back to a specific source publisher, source URL, source version, and retrieval timestamp. User-facing answers must be able to cite their source. The provenance schema (see §10) is required across every entity.
4.3 Versioning never destroys history
Plan-year data, code-set vintages, and policy versions are immutable once ingested. New versions are appended, never overwritten. A service-date query must resolve to the version of the rule, code, or plan that was effective on that date.
4.4 Deterministic before LLM
Field-level facts (code descriptors, deductibles, OOP max, deadlines) are extracted deterministically wherever the source structure allows. LLM-assisted extraction is used only after deterministic methods have been attempted, with explicit confidence scoring and a human review queue for low-confidence outputs.
4.5 Source-of-truth hierarchy
When two sources conflict on a fact, the system flags the conflict and prefers official plan documents (SBC, EOC, plan brochure) over derived PUF data for user-facing explanations. Both values are stored and exposed via the API; the calling application makes the final presentation decision.
4.6 Allowlist-only crawling
Payer policy, SBC, and provider directory crawling operates against an explicit allowlist of source URLs. The system respects robots.txt and ToS, identifies itself via a documented User-Agent, applies polite rate limiting, and maintains a blocklist of sources that have refused access.
4.7 Idempotent, restartable ingestion
Every ingestion job is idempotent: re-running it against an unchanged source produces no diff. Jobs are checkpointed so that partial failures can resume rather than restart. Source artifacts (raw downloads) are persisted before parsing so reprocessing does not require re-fetching.
5. Compliance and legal posture
5.1 AMA CPT license
Full CPT code descriptors are proprietary and licensed by the American Medical Association. This system does not ingest, store, reproduce, or distribute CPT descriptors. The system detects CPT-like 5-digit numeric codes in user-uploaded documents (handled in the separate PHI service), records the code value as observed, and falls back to broad service-category inference using free metadata. Every CPT code response carries a descriptor_source flag with value unavailable_unlicensed and a user-facing caveat. If MASA later acquires a CPT license, a single ingestion module can be added without changes to the data model.
5.2 Payer policy copyright
Aetna Clinical Policy Bulletins, UHC Medical Policies, Cigna Coverage Policies, and similar materials are copyrighted by the issuing payer. The system stores extracted criteria for advocacy-support use under a fair-use reading (transformative use, criticism, advocacy support). The system preserves raw source URLs and last-retrieved dates so users can always be directed to the original. Verbatim reproduction of long passages in user-facing output is avoided in favor of summarization with citation.
5.3 Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) and insurance advice
Generating appeal letters, asserting that a denial is improper, or telling a member that a service should have been covered can cross into UPL and unauthorized insurance advice depending on state and framing. All user-facing outputs that touch these areas must include a disclaimer that the system provides information and self-help support, not legal or licensed insurance advice. Deadline calculations require human review (see §11). The product team must maintain a list of state-by-state framing constraints.
5.4 HIPAA
This system is non-PHI by construction. The companion document-ingestion service that handles user EOBs is the HIPAA-covered surface. The architectural boundary between them is enforced via separate deployment, separate credentials, and one-way API access (PHI service calls this system; this system never calls into PHI infrastructure).
5.5 Crawling, robots.txt, and rate limiting
All crawlers honor robots.txt, identify themselves via a documented User-Agent string of the form MASA-AdvocacyIngestor/1.0 (+contact_url), apply per-host rate limits, back off on 429/503, and respect Crawl-delay directives. Sources that explicitly disallow access are recorded in a blocklist and not retried. The system supports per-source override only with written legal approval.
5.6 User-facing disclaimers (required at the AI application layer)
Information shown is reference data from public sources, not legal or licensed insurance advice.
Plan benefits, deductibles, and OOP amounts shown should be verified against the member's current plan documents.
Network status data may be stale; verify directly with the payer and provider before care.
Appeal deadlines shown are derived from public rules and may not reflect plan-specific or recent regulatory changes; verify before relying on them.
Coverage determinations require the payer's review of the specific claim; this system does not make coverage decisions.
6. Key product use cases
These use cases drive the data requirements that follow. Each one is the responsibility of the downstream AI application; this PRD specifies the data that must be available to serve them.
6.1 Use Case 1 — Explain claim-line codes
User input: EOB or bill contains a code of any supported type — ICD-10-CM, ICD-10-PCS, HCPCS Level II, CPT, revenue code, modifier, place-of-service, NDC, MS-DRG, CARC, or RARC.
System behavior: Detect code type from format. Normalize. Retrieve descriptor and metadata from the appropriate code set. If CPT, return unavailable_unlicensed with category fallback. If CARC/RARC, return human-readable explanation of the denial reason. Link code to benefit category and likely payer policy where applicable.
Example: "A0428 is a HCPCS Level II ambulance transport code for non-emergency basic life support transport. Coverage typically depends on medical necessity, origin/destination, and whether other transportation would have been unsafe."
6.2 Use Case 2 — Identify the user's plan
User input: Plan name, issuer, state, county, ZIP code, metal tier, HIOS plan ID, Medicare Advantage contract/PBP, Medicaid plan, or a plan document.
System behavior: Route to the correct plan database based on plan type. Resolve to a canonical plan identifier. Return plan metadata, materials URLs, and structured benefits.
6.3 Use Case 3 — Explain plan benefits
User question: "Should this have been covered?" or "Why did I owe this much?"
System behavior: Map service to benefit category. Retrieve structured benefit, cost-sharing, deductible, OOP, and prior-auth fields. Cross-reference with SBC if available. For Medicare, cross-reference with the relevant NCD or LCD. Generate a plain-English explanation with citations.
6.4 Use Case 4 — Retrieve and parse SBC or plan document
System behavior: Locate SBC URL from QHP Landscape, Marketplace API, or other plan-material discovery sources. Download the PDF. Extract standardized SBC sections (deductible, OOP max, copay/coinsurance, common medical events, services you may need, excluded services, prior authorization notes, coverage examples). Store raw PDF, extracted text, page-level chunks, and structured fields with per-field confidence scores.
6.5 Use Case 5 — Identify appeal pathway
User input: Denial reason (extracted via CARC/RARC and/or denial letter text), plan type, state, date on denial letter.
System behavior: Determine which appeals framework applies (federal ACA, Medicare 5-level, Medicaid fair hearing, ERISA, NSA IDR, state-specific). Identify the relevant internal vs. external review path. Identify deadline and required next steps. Retrieve the relevant payer medical policy if the denial is medical-necessity, experimental, or PA-related. Generate a checklist and structured appeal package outline.
6.6 Use Case 6 — Ambulance bill triage (MASA wedge)
User input: Ambulance bill with HCPCS code (A0425–A0436 range typically), modifier, charge amount, payer, plan type, and event details (emergency vs. non-emergency, origin, destination).
System behavior: Decode HCPCS and ambulance modifier. Cross-reference with NCD 10.1 medical-necessity criteria. If Medicare or MA, surface Ambulance Fee Schedule rate for the locality. If commercial plan, surface negotiated rate from Hospital Price Transparency or TiC MRF where available. Identify whether the No Surprises Act applies (out-of-network emergency, ground ambulance carve-out specifics, air ambulance protections). Generate an advocacy summary with specific code, modifier, rule citations, and recommended next steps.
6.7 Use Case 7 — Good Faith Estimate dispute (uninsured / self-pay)
User input: Uninsured or self-pay patient received a written Good Faith Estimate (GFE) before a scheduled service, and the final billed amount exceeds the GFE for any single provider by $400 or more.
System behavior: Compare GFE amount(s) to final bill amount(s). Identify whether the $400 threshold for Patient-Provider Dispute Resolution (PPDR) under 45 CFR §149.620 is met. Verify timing: PPDR initiation must occur within 120 calendar days of receipt of the bill. Surface the required GFE content elements (per 45 CFR §149.610) that should have been included, flagging any missing elements as additional advocacy leverage. Identify the convening-provider vs. co-provider distinction if multiple providers are involved. Generate a structured PPDR initiation summary with the citation, the dollar gap, the deadline, the dispute fee, and the SDR (Selected Dispute Resolution entity) submission path.
Why this matters: Use-case analysis showed ~10% of real-world pain centers on quoted-vs-billed price disputes for uninsured/self-pay scenarios. GFE plus PPDR is the federal regulatory mechanism that directly addresses this. Insured-patient GFE provisions have been deferred by HHS pending rulemaking; this PRD covers the uninsured/self-pay rules that are in effect and will track regulatory updates if insured-patient rules are finalized.
7. Data sources by extractor family
Sources are organized into eight families. Each family is implementable as a self-contained module that follows the shared source-registry, ingestion pipeline, normalization, and provenance contracts defined in §§9–10.
Family A — Code-set bulk-file ingestors
This family fetches official code-set release files on a scheduled cadence, parses them into a shared canonical schema, detects added/revised/deleted codes, and exposes lookup and search APIs. All sources in this family are P0.
Family B — Marketplace QHP data ingestor
This family covers ACA Marketplace plan data: the QHP Landscape files, Plan Attributes PUF, Benefits & Cost Sharing PUF, SADP PUF, and the Healthcare.gov Marketplace API. These are the structured backbone for ACA plan lookup, plan-material discovery, and benefit/cost-sharing reasoning. All P0.
Family C — Medicare data ingestor
This family adds Medicare coverage to the plan-side data model. It covers Medicare Advantage and Part D Landscape files, the Medicare Coverage Database (NCDs and LCDs), the Medicare Ambulance Fee Schedule and Physician Fee Schedule, and the Medicare Claims Processing and Benefit Policy Manuals. NCD 10.1 (Ambulance) and the Ambulance Fee Schedule are P0 because of MASA's wedge.
Family D — Medicaid data ingestor
Medicaid is administered state-by-state, so the ingestion approach is necessarily state-by-state. P0 scope is limited to the top 10 states by MASA member density (defined by the product team), focusing on Medicaid Managed Care plan documents and Medicaid appeal/fair-hearing rules. National Medicaid Statistical Information System (T-MSIS) data is out of scope.
Family E — Regulatory rules ingestor
This family ingests appeal rules and balance-billing/surprise-billing rules from federal and state sources. The No Surprises Act is elevated to P0 because of MASA's wedge.
Family F — SBC PDF crawler and parser
SBCs are standardized Summary of Benefits and Coverage documents under the ACA. URLs are discovered via QHP Landscape (medical) and analogous Medicare Advantage and Medicaid sources where applicable. Employer-sponsored SBCs do not have a central registry; for those, this family exposes a parser that operates on user-uploaded PDFs (called from the separate PHI service).
Family G — Payer medical policy crawler
Payer medical policies define when services are medically necessary, experimental, or subject to prior authorization. Critical for medical-necessity denial advocacy. P1 scope is the top 7 national payers; expand to regional payers and BCBS state plans in P2.
Family H — Provider and network data ingestor
Family I — Price transparency (ambulance HCPCS only)
Hospital Price Transparency files and payer Transparency in Coverage MRFs are deferred at the full-dataset level due to operational cost, but a narrow slice — restricted to ambulance HCPCS codes — is included to enable negotiated-rate context in ambulance bill advocacy. Scope: top-200 U.S. hospitals by ambulance volume (HPT) and top-5 national payers (TiC), filtered to A0021–A0999 HCPCS codes.

10.2 Standard ingestion flow
Read source registry entry.
Fetch source file or API response.
Store raw artifact in object storage with SHA-256 hash.
Compare hash against last successful run; skip if unchanged.
Detect schema; fail fast if schema-version mismatch.
Parse into staging tables.
Validate required columns and field types.
Normalize fields (currency to cents, dates to ISO 8601 UTC, codes to canonical form, URLs validated).
Run entity matching against existing records.
Append a new DatasetRelease record; never overwrite prior versions.
Publish to production tables (transactional swap or versioned read alias).
Build/refresh search index entries.
Generate ingestion report: row counts, diff vs. prior, anomalies, errors, time elapsed.
Alert on threshold breaches (see §11).

11.2 Human review queue triggers
Items are routed to the review queue when:
Appeal deadline is extracted from unstructured legal text.
Balance-billing rule is extracted from state statute.
SBC extraction confidence is below threshold on any required field.
Payer medical policy criteria will be used in user-facing appeal generation.
Network status is ambiguous (e.g., NPI appears in one directory but not another).
Source data conflicts across PUF, SBC, and issuer plan materials.
Plan year mismatch exists between plan material and structured data.
Coverage determination text changes meaningfully between versions.
Schema drift is detected on a stable source.

12. AI / LLM requirements
12.1 Where LLM is used in this layer
Extracting structured criteria from unstructured payer policy and rule text after deterministic methods have been attempted.
Classifying SBC sections and tables.
Generating concise, citation-anchored summaries of long medical policies and rule text for the AI application to consume.
Mapping messy benefit_name strings to canonical benefit taxonomy.
Parsing low-quality SBC PDFs after native and OCR extraction have produced low-confidence results.
12.2 Where LLM is NOT the sole source
LLMs must not be the sole source for any of the following:
Code descriptors (use the official code-set ingestion).
Plan deductibles, OOP maxima, copay/coinsurance amounts (use PUFs and SBCs).
Appeal deadlines (human-reviewed structured rules only).
Coverage determinations (use NCDs/LCDs and plan documents).

14. Eval and test framework
Because user-facing advocacy outputs will get cited back to payers, members, and possibly regulators, every scraper and every API endpoint must have an automated test suite. Claude Code is expected to scaffold these alongside the extractors.
14.1 Test categories
Schema tests — every parser is exercised against fixture files captured from real source releases; field-by-field equality asserted.
Drift tests — when a new source release is ingested, a drift report flags fields that changed value in a way inconsistent with normal year-over-year evolution.
Lookup tests — golden test cases per code set (e.g., A0428 → 'BLS non-emergency transport'); regression-tested on every build.
Plan lookup tests — fixtures of state+county+plan_year queries with expected plan ID sets.
SBC extraction tests — a fixture set of representative SBC PDFs with manually-verified extracted field values; field-level accuracy thresholds enforced.
Appeal rule tests — golden cases of denial scenario + plan type + state → expected appeal pathway and deadline.
GFE / PPDR tests — fixture cases of GFE amounts + final bills + dates → expected PPDR eligibility, threshold gap, days remaining, and current SDR fee.
Ambulance triage end-to-end tests — fixture EOB lines (synthesized, non-PHI) with HCPCS + modifier + payer + state, expected to produce a known triage output.
14.2 Eval cadence
Per-build: full schema and lookup test suites.
Per-source-release: drift tests against the new release.
Weekly: full extraction-confidence sample audit against the human review queue.
Monthly: end-to-end triage eval against the golden ambulance fixture set.
15. Risks and mitigations
Risk
Impact
Mitigation
CPT licensing limitation
High
Do not ingest CPT descriptors; CPT detection-only with category fallback. License later if business case supports it.
SBC PDF extraction errors
High
Cascade extraction; per-field confidence; human review queue; preserve raw PDF with hash for citation.
Plan ID join failures across PUF + Landscape + SBC
High
Normalize HIOS to canonical 14-char form; multi-key matching on issuer + state + plan_year + variant; explicit unmatched-row report.
PUF vs. SBC conflict on a benefit value
Medium
Store both; flag conflict; default to SBC for user-facing explanation (issuer-published material outranks derived PUF).
Provider directory staleness
High
Display staleness warning in every API response; recommend payer/provider verification before care; do not use stale directory data as the sole basis for a network claim.
Appeal deadline extraction errors
High
Human review required for every deadline before user-facing publication; user-facing output always includes a 'verify the deadline' caveat.
Payer policy fragmentation
Medium
Start with top 7 payers and high-frequency denial categories. Expand opportunistically.
State rule variability
Medium
Start federal; layer in top-10 states. Track gaps explicitly.
NSA ground-ambulance carve-out misinterpretation
High
Model the carve-out explicitly with category-specific flags; surface state-specific protections that fill the gap.
GFE rule volatility (PPDR fee, SDR list, potential insured-patient expansion)
Medium
Quarterly refresh on GFE family; track effective date on every dollar value; preserve historical fee versions for retrospective dispute calculations.
GFE deadline miscalculation (120-day PPDR initiation)
High
Human review on every deadline value before user-facing publication; user-facing output always carries a 'verify with current CMS guidance' caveat; deadline math expressed as a verifiable calculation showing inputs.
Crawler blocked by source
Medium
Maintain blocklist; do not retry blocked sources; route to manual export workflow if business-critical.
Payer policy copyright exposure
Medium
User-facing output uses summarization with citation, not verbatim long passages; preserve source URL for redirect.
UPL / unauthorized insurance advice
High
Disclaimers required at application layer; deadline calculations require human review; advocacy framing reviewed by counsel.
Price transparency volume
Medium
Ambulance-HCPCS scoping makes volume tractable; consider further narrowing to top-3 payers if needed.
Source URL drift (CMS reorganizations)
Medium
Source registry centralizes URLs; weekly URL-health check; archive copy of every ingested file via SHA-256 retention.
